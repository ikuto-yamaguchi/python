# CAP-GEN-002-IAS-001: invariant auxiliary symmetry certificate

## Why this direction

GAS-001 showed that a compact passive latent-action representation can be worse over its
lifetime when it leaves a large control-alignment symmetry. The next question is whether
an auxiliary observation genuinely removes that symmetry or merely acts as an
environment-specific tag.

This note begins from prior art rather than treating invariance as new:

- nonlinear ICA with auxiliary variables proves identifiability under explicit variation
  assumptions (arXiv:1805.08651);
- invariant causal prediction and invariant risk minimization use stability across
  environments (arXiv:1501.01332, arXiv:1907.02893);
- IRM can fail when observed environments do not expose the relevant spurious variation
  (arXiv:2010.05761);
- causal representation learning with unknown interventions gives identifiability only
  under stated intervention and diversity assumptions (arXiv:2306.00542 and successors);
- automorphism groups and color-preserving stabilizers are standard mathematics.

IAS-001 does not claim any of those results as novel.

## Finite setting

Let a shared latent-action quotient have action set `A` and automorphism group `Gamma`.
An auxiliary channel produces an action-conditioned signature in each observed
environment:

```text
Q_e(x | a)
```

The exact deterministic gate stores one signature per action and environment. A channel
is certified only over the observed environments when its signature is unchanged for
every action:

```text
Q_e(. | a) = Q_e'(. | a) for every observed e, e', a.
```

For a certified channel bundle `V`, the residual symmetry is

```text
Gamma_V = { gamma in Gamma : Q_V(. | a) = Q_V(. | gamma(a)) for every a }.
```

The latent-to-control alignment is unique within the assumed finite model exactly when
`Gamma_V` contains only the identity.

This is a color-preserving automorphism statement, not a new group-theoretic theorem.

## Theorem boundary 1: information is not grounding

A channel can assign a unique tag to every action in every training environment and still
fail to provide stable semantics when the tag-to-action codebook changes with policy or
environment.

The gate constructs an eight-action cycle. A changing unique tag has three bits of
training information under a uniform action prior and makes every environment look
individually asymmetric. A naive environment-by-environment score therefore reports one
remaining alignment.

However, the tag changes across environments, so it is rejected by the finite invariance
certificate. The certified residual group remains the full dihedral group of order 16.

Therefore high mutual information, perfect training classification, or a trivial
per-environment stabilizer is insufficient evidence of grounded identity.

## Theorem boundary 2: low-information stable evidence can be stronger

Two stable binary marker channels identify two adjacent vertices of the same eight-cycle.
Their combined deterministic information is only about 1.061 bits under a uniform action
prior. Nevertheless, fixing the two adjacent positions removes every non-identity
rotation and reflection, so the residual group is trivial.

Thus the correct objective is not to maximize auxiliary information. It is to minimize
residual control ambiguity under a validated stability assumption.

## Theorem boundary 3: finite observed invariance is not universal invariance

A channel may be identical in every currently observed environment and change in the next
one. IAS-001 includes this prospective counterexample.

Accordingly, `certified` always means certified over the supplied environment family.
Without assumptions about how future environments are generated, no finite collection of
observations proves universal invariance. Prospective environments can falsify a channel;
they cannot logically certify every possible future regime.

## Resource objective

For an auxiliary bundle `V`, the project should account for

```text
K_aux(V)
C_validate(V)
C_infer(V)
M_peak(V)
Gamma_V
tau(Gamma_V)
```

where `tau` is the remaining direct-interaction symmetry tax from GAS-001. A simple scalar
example uses

```text
J(V) = K_aux(V) + direct_anchor_bits * base_size(Gamma_V).
```

In the frozen gate:

- no auxiliary: 0 executable bits + 14 symmetry-tax bits = 14;
- changing unique tag: 3 executable bits + 14 symmetry-tax bits = 17;
- two stable markers: 8 executable bits + 0 symmetry-tax bits = 8.

A high-information training tag is therefore worse than a lower-information stable
bundle once residual grounding cost is charged.

The full research objective remains vector-valued. Validation operations, inference
operations, memory, data support, and interaction risk must not be silently converted to
free quantities.

## New hypothesis candidate

The unverified candidate is a shared executable auxiliary grammar learned from one raw
mixed interaction stream that jointly proposes:

1. candidate auxiliary spans and parsers;
2. latent policy/environment segmentation;
3. action-conditioned auxiliary mechanisms;
4. prospective invariance tests;
5. residual automorphism groups;
6. a minimum-lifetime bundle of auxiliary probes and direct interactions.

The grammar should be selected by certified orbit reduction per complete lifetime
resource, not by reconstruction loss or information gain alone.

## Stagnation guard

The next step must not add more marker channels or larger cycles. It must address one of:

- unknown environment boundaries;
- noisy finite-sample invariance rather than exact tables;
- an admissible lower bound for auxiliary-bundle search;
- a natural/public interaction stream;
- transfer of the same learned certificate to multiple CAP-GEN axes.

A further synthetic example that only changes colors, action count, or graph size is not
research progress.
