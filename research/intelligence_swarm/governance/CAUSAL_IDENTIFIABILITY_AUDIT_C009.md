## Question under audit

Can raw language equivalence identify a latent intervention partition when the utterances are observationally reducible to an environment/intervention index?

This cycle addresses one of the explicit blockers left by C008: construct an exact negative example rather than relying only on an information-theoretic slogan.

## Counterexample: complete paired trajectories plus index-reducible language do not identify the intervention partition

### Model A

Let the latent state be two binary variables

\[
Z=(A,B)\in\{0,1\}^2.
\]

The observation is generated by an unknown invertible map

\[
X=f(A,B).
\]

The available intervention environment `e=1` applies the deterministic transition

\[
(A,B)\mapsto(A\oplus 1,B\oplus 1),
\]

while `e=0` leaves the state unchanged. Assume the dataset contains complete paired pre/post observations `(X_before, X_after, e)`.

Under this parameterization, the intervention target is the two-variable set `{A,B}`.

### Model B

Define an alternative invertible latent parameterization

\[
C=A,\qquad D=A\oplus B.
\]

Its inverse is

\[
A=C,\qquad B=C\oplus D.
\]

Define

\[
g(C,D)=f(C,C\oplus D).
\]

The same observed transition is then represented as

\[
(C,D)\mapsto(C\oplus 1,D).
\]

Under this parameterization, the intervention target is the one-variable set `{C}`.

### Observational equivalence

For every latent state and every environment, Models A and B generate exactly the same paired observations:

\[
(X_{before},X_{after},e).
\]

The maps between `(A,B)` and `(C,D)` are bijective, and `g` absorbs the reparameterization. Therefore no estimator using the complete paired non-language trajectory can distinguish:

- a two-target intervention on the partition `{A},{B}`; from
- a one-target intervention on the partition `{C},{D}`.

The disagreement is not merely a variable-name permutation. The intervention support cardinality and the latent partition semantics differ.

## Why raw utterances do not help when they reduce to an index

Let the utterance be generated only from the observed environment index:

\[
L=\ell(e),
\]

where `ell` may produce arbitrarily rich natural-language strings, paraphrases, or compositional-looking surface forms, but contains no information beyond `e`.

Then

\[
\sigma(X_{before},X_{after},e,L)=\sigma(X_{before},X_{after},e).
\]

Equivalently, for every candidate mechanism abstraction `M`,

\[
I(M;L\mid X_{before},X_{after},e)=0.
\]

Thus adding the raw utterance cannot break the equivalence between Models A and B. Correct trajectory-language pairing and language shuffle can detect dependence on `e`, but cannot determine whether the true intervention target is `{A,B}` or `{C}`.

This remains true even if:

- every pre/post state is observed without noise;
- the observation map is invertible;
- the utterance vocabulary is unlimited;
- each environment has many paraphrases;
- a language encoder has unlimited capacity;
- the full history, reward, action, time, and policy phase are available.

The missing ingredient is not model capacity. It is an independently separating intervention or a language relation whose information is not measurable with respect to the environment index.

## General impossibility lemma

Let `X` denote the complete non-language record and `E` the observed environment/intervention index. If

\[
L=h(E,\epsilon_L)
\]

with language noise `epsilon_L` conditionally independent of the latent mechanism model given `E`, then

\[
M\perp L\mid X,E
\]

for every mechanism variable `M` whose only connection to `L` is through `E`.

Consequently, any two causal models that are observationally equivalent under `(X,E)` remain observationally equivalent under `(X,E,L)`. Raw language cannot refine the equivalence class unless it carries mechanism information not already contained in the index.

This is a negative result, not a new intelligence mechanism.

## Prior-art boundary refinement

### Multi-view nonlinear ICA — Gresele et al., UAI 2020

Primary source:

- https://proceedings.mlr.press/v115/gresele20a.html

Multiple sufficiently different noisy views can identify shared independent sources under the paper's assumptions. Therefore treating utterances and trajectories as two views is not, by itself, a novel route around the counterexample. The counterexample deliberately makes language a view of `E`, not an independently corrupted view of the latent mechanism variables.

### Hidden Markov nonlinear ICA — Hälvä & Hyvärinen, UAI 2020

Primary source:

- https://proceedings.mlr.press/v124/halva20a.html

A latent regime can replace a manually supplied segment index and still yield identifiability under the model assumptions. Therefore novelty cannot be recovered merely by hiding the environment label and inferring it from language. If language only reveals the same regime, the scientific content remains regime-indexed identifiability.

### Mechanistic Independence — Matthes, Han & Shen, ICLR 2026

Primary source:

- https://openreview.net/forum?id=0VVdai71xb

This work identifies latent subspaces through how factors act on observations, including support-, sparsity-, and higher-order mechanistic criteria under nonlinear and non-invertible mixing. Hence a future T1 theorem must also differ from a result in which language merely exposes intervention support or mechanistic independence already sufficient for subspace recovery.

### Binary latent causal graphical models — Lee & Gu, TMLR 2026

Primary source:

- https://openreview.net/forum?id=KiiSlAsLuN

The double-triangular measurement condition provides identifiability for binary latent causal graphical models with heterogeneous observed variables. Language as another heterogeneous measurement channel is therefore not automatically novel; the claimed refinement must survive comparison with measurement-model identifiability conditions.

## Consequence for RQ-001-T1

The broad possibility

> raw utterances refine trajectory-only causal equivalence

is false without a non-index-reducibility assumption.

The surviving theory-only candidate must explicitly require that no statistic sufficient for the language contribution is measurable solely from the environment/domain index. One possible necessary condition is:

\[
I(M;L\mid X,E)>0,
\]

where `M` is independently defined by intervention effects rather than researcher-assigned language labels.

Even this is not sufficient. A positive theorem must additionally show that the language information removes a specific causal-model symmetry rather than merely predicting the environment or intervention support.

## Decision

### RQ-001-T1: NARROWED AGAIN — NOT ADOPTED

Retained candidate:

> Characterize when relational constraints among raw utterances that are not measurable from the environment/intervention index strictly refine a causal-model equivalence remaining after complete non-language trajectories; prove impossibility for index-reducible language and for intervention families that do not separate competing latent partitions.

The candidate is not adopted because:

- only the negative construction is now established;
- no nontrivial positive construction exists;
- no formal sufficient condition has been proved;
- novelty beyond auxiliary-variable nonlinear ICA, multi-view nonlinear ICA, hidden-regime identifiability, mechanistic-independence identifiability, and heterogeneous measurement-model identifiability is not established;
- empirical Gate I remains rejected;
- R0.1 public capability reproduction remains incomplete.
