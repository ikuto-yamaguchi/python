## Decision-relevant question

C089 established that language-internal topic or predictive-state identification does not orient the language quotient relative to the external target quotient. It narrowed the positive path to preregistered cross-system observations.

The question for C090 is:

> Do paired multimodal or multi-view contrastive observations already identify the relative language–world alignment needed for `d`, and under which assumptions does recovery of a shared latent factor fail to identify raw-language equivalence, intervention-target partition, or denotation?

## Primary prior art

### Identifiability Results for Multimodal Contrastive Learning

Daunhawer, Bizeul, Palumbo, Marx, and Vogt (ICLR 2023) distinguish multi-view data generated by one mechanism from multimodal data generated by distinct modality-specific mechanisms. Under their stated generative, smoothness, dependence, and contrastive-learning assumptions, shared latent factors can be block-identified even when shared and modality-specific factors exhibit nontrivial dependencies.

This is direct prior art against any broad claim that paired heterogeneous modalities cannot identify shared latent content, or that contrastive pairing of language and another modality is intrinsically incapable of recovering a common factor.

The guarantee is nevertheless a shared-factor guarantee. It does not by itself state that the shared block equals the exact controlled target quotient `P`, that each raw-language equivalence class is one shared factor, or that the recovered coordinate has an externally fixed semantic orientation.

### Multi-View Causal Representation Learning with Partial Observability

Yao et al. (ICLR 2024) study simultaneously observed views in which each view is a nonlinear mixture of a subset of causally related latent variables. They prove that information shared across subsets of views can be learned up to a smooth bijection and provide graphical criteria—an identifiability algebra—for which latent subsets are recoverable from view overlap.

This removes another overly broad novelty claim: multiple partially observed views can identify finer shared latent representations under explicit overlap and generative assumptions. Partial observability is not automatically an identifiability obstruction.

Again, the theorem identifies latent information shared by declared views. It does not independently establish that the view-incidence pattern is semantic-label-free, that the shared subset equals the intervention-target quotient, or that a language token/class denotes a particular target block rather than an episode, context, speaker, policy, or object-instance factor shared by the paired samples.

### Earlier multi-view nonlinear ICA boundary

Gresele et al. (UAI 2020) show that jointly observed sufficiently different noisy views can identify common independent latent sources under their multi-view nonlinear ICA assumptions even though each view alone is non-identifiable. C067 already used unpaired shared-component analysis to separate marginal alignment from class coupling; C090 adds the paired nonlinear/multimodal positive side.

Therefore the remaining candidate novelty cannot be “paired cross-modal views recover shared information.” It must concern whether the actual grounded-causal observation law singles out the semantic quotient and denotation rather than some other shared latent block.

## Theorem / assumption / guarantee comparison

| Object | Observations | Key assumptions | Guarantee | Not guaranteed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Multimodal contrastive identifiability (Daunhawer et al.) | paired samples from heterogeneous modalities | declared shared/private latent generative structure; suitable smooth/invertible mechanisms; contrastive population objective and regularity assumptions | shared latent block up to the theorem's equivalence class / block transformation | that the shared block is `P`; cardinality or functional form of `d`; external semantic orientation |
| Multi-view CRL with partial observability (Yao et al.) | simultaneously observed partial views and known view grouping | each view mixes a subset of latent variables; graphical overlap conditions; suitable nonlinear encoders/objective | shared information across selected view subsets up to smooth bijection; graphical recoverability criteria | target-ID-free view construction; exact full-consequence quotient; language equivalence; denotation assertions |
| Multi-view nonlinear ICA (Gresele et al.) | paired noisy views of a common source | independent components and sufficiently different component-wise corruptions / view mechanisms | common source components up to specified component-wise ambiguities | semantic target ontology or world-facing names |
| C087–C089 governed object | language law, controlled action/consequence law, and explicit cross-system observations | exact quotient estimand; structural merge only; declared context and denotation form | identified set of `(Q,P,d)` and invariant must/cannot/may relations | point completion from contrastive similarity alone |

The central distinction is:

1. a paired-view theorem can identify a common latent variable or block;
2. grounding requires proving that this common block is the declared semantic relation between language classes and exact controlled target blocks;
3. that proof fails if the pairing variable also contains episode identity, context, speaker, policy, object instance, environment, or any other shared nuisance;
4. a smooth-bijection or block-identification guarantee does not by itself fix a discrete denotation map or external semantic orientation.

## Proposition C090.1: shared-factor identification is not denotation identification

Assume paired observations `(L,W)` are generated by

- a shared latent `S`,
- language-private latent `U_L`,
- world-private latent `U_W`,
- modality mechanisms `L=f_L(S,U_L)` and `W=f_W(S,U_W)`.

Assume a valid multimodal identifiability theorem recovers `S` up to its allowed smooth or block transformation.

This does not identify `d : Q -> P` unless additional assumptions establish all of the following:

1. `Q` is a measurable quotient of the recovered shared factor rather than a language-private or mixed factor;
2. `P` is the exact full-consequence controlled quotient of the recovered shared factor rather than a world-private or mixed factor;
3. context, episode, policy, speaker, environment, and object-instance variables cannot explain the cross-view dependence that identifies `S`;
4. the admissible transformation of `S` preserves or uniquely determines the discrete class relation required by `d`;
5. no alternative `(Q,P,d)` completion yields the same paired law.

Proof sketch:

- A theorem may correctly recover the maximal shared information `S`.
- Let `S=(T,C)`, where `T` is target-relevant and `C` is a paired context or episode variable.
- A representation identifying the full shared block `(T,C)` satisfies the theorem.
- One candidate semantic quotient groups by `T`; another groups by `(T,C)`; a third may map language classes through a context-indexed relation `d(q,c)`.
- If the declared observations do not independently state which quotient is semantic, all candidates can preserve the paired data law and contrastive optimum.
- Therefore shared-block identification is insufficient for point identification of `Q`, `P`, or `d`.

This is a guarantee-boundary result, not a new intelligence principle.

## Counterexample 1: episode identity is the easiest shared factor

Let each paired episode have a unique nuisance identifier `E`. The language view contains a stylistic marker determined by `E`; the world view contains a background texture or timestamp determined by the same `E`. The intended target `T` also affects both views but more weakly.

A contrastive method can perfectly match paired samples by recovering `E`. At population level, `E` is genuinely shared information, and a theorem that identifies the shared latent block can be satisfied.

Nevertheless:

- two utterances concerning the same target but different episodes remain separated;
- two targets appearing in the same episode can be spuriously associated;
- the recovered factor is not the exact target quotient `P`;
- no denotation relation between language classes and intervention blocks is identified.

Therefore pair retrieval accuracy, contrastive loss, and shared-factor recovery are not direct `d` metrics.

## Counterexample 2: paired target instance does not identify target type

Suppose each pair contains language about one physical object instance and observations of that same instance. Let `I` denote instance identity and `T` denote controlled target type.

Both modalities share `(I,T)`. A valid shared representation may identify instance-specific content. Yet the canonical target quotient `P` groups states by complete controlled response law and may merge multiple instances of the same type.

Two compatible semantic models remain:

- type semantics: utterances denote the target quotient block `T`;
- instance semantics: utterances denote the paired object instance `I`.

If held-out interventions across instances are absent, the paired law does not decide which ontology is intended. Thus same-instance pairing can be leakage-like supervision for retrieval while leaving the type-level target quotient unresolved.

## Counterexample 3: smooth-bijection identifiability does not fix a discrete partition

Let a shared continuous factor `S` be identified only up to a smooth bijection `h(S)`. Define a candidate language equivalence by thresholding `S` at zero, and a target partition by thresholding the physical response at the corresponding boundary.

Under a generic smooth bijection, the numeric boundary and geometry can move. The continuous shared information remains identified up to the theorem's equivalence, but a particular externally chosen discrete partition is not invariant unless the partition is defined intrinsically by the controlled law and its boundary is preserved by every admissible transformation.

Therefore:

> identification up to a smooth bijection does not automatically identify a named or thresholded semantic class partition.

The correct evaluation must use invariants of the allowed equivalence class or add an independent controlled observation that fixes the quotient.

## Counterexample 4: pair construction can encode the answer

Suppose positive pairs are produced by first reading a gold target ID and then selecting one utterance and one world trajectory with that same ID. The learner never receives the ID explicitly, but the positive-pair graph is generated from it.

A contrastive model may then recover the target classes exactly. This is a valid supervised signal transmitted through pair selection, not unsupervised discovery from raw interaction.

Hence the following must be audited as potential semantic leakage:

- positive-pair construction using target IDs or target masks;
- negative sampling constrained by semantic class;
- dataset splits preserving target-specific templates;
- captions generated from simulator variable or object names;
- view-incidence graphs created from the gold ontology;
- same-target batching or evaluator-derived pairing.

A pairing rule is admissible for grounding only if its physical generation mechanism is independently observable and could be executed without reading `Q`, `P`, `d`, parser slots, or simulator semantic names.

## Counterexample 5: shared information can be too coarse

Let two target blocks `p1,p2` generate distinct controlled consequences but the language view only states a superordinate instruction applying to either target. The paired views share only a coarse factor `S={p1 or p2}` while the world-private factor distinguishes the exact target.

A multi-view method can correctly identify all information shared between language and world, yet cannot recover the finer exact target partition or a point denotation. The limitation is not optimization failure: the missing distinction is not shared by the declared views.

This rules out the inference that better multimodal contrastive training must eventually recover every world target distinction.

## Admissibility contract for paired cross-system evidence

A paired observation law may shrink the joint `(Q,P,d)` identified set only after all of the following are recorded:

1. **Physical pairing mechanism**: how the utterance and world consequence become paired without consulting semantic labels.
2. **Shared/private variable declaration**: which nuisance factors can be shared across views and which are excluded or randomized.
3. **Target estimand link**: why the shared world factor equals, refines, or coarsens the C087 exact full-consequence quotient.
4. **Language estimand link**: why the shared language factor equals raw-language equivalence rather than speaker, register, episode, or template identity.
5. **Transformation invariance**: which assertions about `Q/P/d` survive the theorem's permutation, component-wise, block, or smooth-bijection ambiguity.
6. **Cardinality and context form**: whether `d` is bijective, many-to-one, one-to-many, or context-indexed.
7. **Negative controls**: episode shuffle, target-instance shuffle, nuisance-preserving pair shuffle, and gold-pairing leakage control.
8. **Direct metrics**: must/cannot/may-denote relations and target quotient relations, not retrieval or representation similarity alone.

Without this contract, paired contrastive data are a baseline signal but not identification evidence.

## Consequence for RQ-001

The remaining candidate RQ is narrowed to:

> Given separately identified or partially identified `Q` and exact full-consequence controlled `P`, which physically generated, semantic-label-free paired or multi-view observation laws make the shared latent invariant coincide with the required language–target relation, and which transformations, nuisance variables, cardinality mismatches, and context dependencies leave multiple denotation completions observationally equivalent?

Novelty is not established. Paired multimodal shared-factor recovery and partial-view identifiability are prior art. A publishable positive result must prove that the benchmark's actual pairing mechanism identifies an invariant of `d` beyond those theorems, or construct a sharp joint identified set showing exactly which denotation assertions remain invariant.
