## Primary work newly audited

### On the Identifiability of Causal Abstractions

Xiusi Li, Sékou-Oumar Kaba, and Siamak Ravanbakhsh, AISTATS 2025, PMLR 258:3241–3249.

Primary sources:

- PMLR record: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v258/li25g.html
- paper PDF: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v258/main/assets/li25g/li25g.pdf
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10834
- OpenReview: https://openreview.net/forum?id=RKiOGRrABL

The paper studies weakly supervised causal representation learning from paired observations `(x, x_tilde)` generated before and after a random intervention whose target set is unobserved. Unlike earlier full-identification results requiring every latent node to be individually intervened on with nonzero probability, it allows arbitrary multi-node intervention target sets and derives the causal abstraction that remains identifiable.

The audited primary records expose no official implementation repository. The work is theorem-driven and reports computing infrastructure and experimental assets as not applicable. Therefore no public baseline reproduction was started in this cycle.

## Exact theorem boundary relevant to RQ-001

Let `I*` be the support of latent intervention target sets. For each target set, consider its non-descendant set in the ground-truth latent DAG. The family of these non-descendant sets generates a sigma-algebra, and its atoms induce a partition of latent nodes.

The paper's main result identifies the latent causal model only up to the quotient graph induced by that partition:

`G = G* / P(sigma(nd(I*)))`.

Informally, two latent nodes that cannot be distinguished by their membership pattern across the non-descendant sets of available interventions remain merged in the identifiable abstraction. When the intervention family separates nodes sufficiently, the partition can become fine; when it does not, only a coarser causal abstraction is identifiable.

This is stronger than merely saying that unknown intervention targets create ambiguity. It supplies a graphical criterion for the finest abstraction supported by the intervention family under the paper's assumptions.

## Prior-art matrix refinement

The following broad claims are no longer admissible as novelty claims:

- unknown multi-node intervention targets imply that no causal representation is identifiable;
- discovering a coarse intervention partition from paired transitions is itself new;
- grouping latent mechanisms according to common intervention response is a language-specific contribution;
- language is useful because it recovers the same target grouping already induced by the intervention family;
- improved target attribution or prediction demonstrates a finer causal partition than the intervention-induced quotient.

The paper already characterises a causal abstraction induced by the available unknown interventions. Any language-based claim must therefore be expressed relative to that quotient.

## Counterexample: language that names the intervention-induced quotient adds no identifiability

Let `A(X, X_tilde)` denote the maximally identifiable causal abstraction under the intervention family in the audited theorem class. Let `L` be a raw utterance generated according to

`L ~ p(L | A(X, X_tilde))`.

Assume that every language statistic used by the learner is conditionally independent of the unresolved microscopic partition `P_micro` given the identifiable abstraction:

`L ⟂ P_micro | A(X, X_tilde)`.

Now take two admissible latent causal models `M1` and `M2` that:

1. induce the same joint law of paired observations;
2. have the same quotient abstraction `A`;
3. differ only in how one quotient block is split into microscopic latent targets.

Because the language channel depends only on `A`, both models also induce exactly the same joint law

`p(X, X_tilde, L)`.

The utterances may consistently name blocks, describe intervention effects, support paraphrases, and improve prediction. Nevertheless, they cannot select between the two microscopic partitions. A language encoder can recover the already-identifiable quotient perfectly while the latent intervention partition remains non-identifiable.

Therefore:

> Language that is measurable with respect to, or conditionally generated from, the intervention-induced identifiable abstraction cannot strictly refine the latent intervention-target partition.

A shuffle gap does not alter this conclusion. Shuffling `L` can reduce task performance because language helps decode the quotient label, while still supplying zero information about how a quotient block should be split.

## Stronger necessary condition

For language to improve population identifiability beyond the audited theorem boundary, there must exist at least two admissible models that agree on the paired-observation law and on the intervention-induced quotient but are separated by the language law:

`p_M1(L | X, X_tilde) != p_M2(L | X, X_tilde)`

on a set of positive measure.

Equivalently, language must carry information about the residual microscopic equivalence class after conditioning on the maximally identifiable non-language abstraction. A necessary information-theoretic condition is

`I(P_residual ; L | A(X, X_tilde)) > 0`.

This condition is not sufficient: the language channel and latent partition may still be jointly recoded. Sufficiency additionally requires an externally fixed denotational or intervention anchor that is invariant across admissible model recodings.

## Consequence for interactive language grounding

Interaction alone does not evade the boundary. If every response is generated from the current identifiable abstraction, action history, environment ID, or completed observable trajectory, then the transcript remains measurable with respect to non-language information and cannot refine the residual microscopic partition.

An interactive protocol can help only if its queries elicit externally anchored distinctions whose response distribution differs across models that remain equivalent under the paired-observation theorem. This must be preregistered as an observation assumption, not inferred retrospectively from task success.

## Consequence for language dynamics pretraining

Pretraining a model to predict language-conditioned transitions can improve finite-sample prediction without improving identifiability. If the transition target and utterance both depend only on the intervention-induced quotient, perfect next-state prediction is compatible with multiple microscopic target partitions.

Accordingly, next-state loss, action accuracy, task success, paraphrase consistency, or language-shuffle degradation cannot by themselves establish a refinement beyond `P(sigma(nd(I*)))`.

## Updated admissible RQ

The remaining candidate is narrowed to:

> After first quotienting the latent causal model by the finest abstraction identifiable from the available unknown intervention family, can an externally anchored population language channel strictly refine a residual quotient block and jointly identify raw-utterance equivalence classes with the refined intervention-target partition, under a theorem that rules out joint recoding of language, latent variables, and intervention semantics?

This formulation is **not adopted**. It remains a preregistration candidate only.

## Adoption requirements added by C020

Before adoption, the candidate must provide:

1. an explicit non-language baseline equivalence class equal to or stronger than the intervention-induced quotient from Li et al. 2025;
2. a proof that the proposed language channel is not measurable with respect to that quotient, paired observations, environment identity, action history, outcome, or completed trajectory;
3. a positive-measure separation condition for language laws across models that remain equivalent after non-language quotienting;
4. an anti-recoding anchor fixing the semantics of at least one residual distinction;
5. a theorem proving strict refinement of at least one quotient block;
6. an impossibility theorem when the residual-information or anchor condition is removed;
7. a direct ablation comparing non-language quotient recovery, quotient-label language, and genuinely residual language;
8. unseen utterance form, composition, target combination, and system splits;
9. public baseline reproduction and preregistration before any new architecture;
10. model bytes, peak RSS, training wall time, CPU inference latency, raw logs, checksums, and seeds `1/7/19` once an experiment begins.

## Decision on RQ-001

### Decision: NARROWED TO LANGUAGE-BASED STRICT REFINEMENT BEYOND THE INTERVENTION-INDUCED MAXIMAL CAUSAL ABSTRACTION — NOT ADOPTED

The broad idea that language helps discover target groups under unknown interventions overlaps with an existing theorem-level characterisation of the causal abstraction already identifiable from the intervention family. Language must demonstrably refine the residual quotient, rather than name, predict, or reconstruct it.

No experiment or architecture is authorised by this result.
