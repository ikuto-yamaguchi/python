## Primary work newly audited

### Grounding Language to Entities and Dynamics for Generalization in Reinforcement Learning

Austin W. Hanjie, Victor Zhong, and Karthik Narasimhan. ICML 2021.

Primary records:

- PMLR: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/hanjie21a.html
- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.07393
- official repository: https://github.com/ahjwang/messenger-emma
- audited repository commit: `8b1b767e01cc23cb7b2cb6c184cde2d8c6b7b49a`

The paper introduces Messenger, a multi-task grid environment in which a free-form manual describes entity identities and environment dynamics. EMMA learns an entity-conditioned cross-modal attention policy from observations, manuals, actions, and environment reward. The work is close to RQ-001 because the language-to-world mapping is not supplied as an explicit entity lexicon and must support held-out combinations.

## RQ variables

Let:

- `U` be raw manual expressions;
- `E` be rendered entity symbols and state features;
- `D` be latent environment dynamics or role assignments;
- `A` be actions;
- `R` be environment reward and terminal success;
- `Q` be the unknown raw-language equivalence relation;
- `P` be the unknown latent intervention-target partition;
- `d: U/Q -> P` be the denotation map required by RQ-001.

## Observations available in Messenger

The learner receives:

- a finite grid observation with entity channels;
- a free-form manual sampled for the episode;
- a fixed action alphabet: up, down, left, right, stay;
- transition observations after actions;
- terminal or step reward;
- train, validation, and test splits over games and combinations;
- stage-specific curricula and environment generation rules.

The learner is not directly given a word-to-entity dictionary. However, the benchmark supplies a highly structured world interface, fixed entity inventory, fixed action semantics, generated game families, and reward-defined task orientation.

## Assumptions embedded in the benchmark

Successful latent grounding relies on at least the following:

1. manuals truthfully describe the current game often enough to guide control;
2. the set of relevant entities and roles is finite and represented in the observation interface;
3. entity symbols remain stable within an episode and are generated by a fixed benchmark schema;
4. reward and termination distinguish successful from unsuccessful behavior;
5. train and test games share the benchmark's compositional grammar and world-generation law;
6. action semantics are known and fixed across all games;
7. the policy class can align entity-conditioned text attention with visual entity channels;
8. held-out combinations do not introduce a fundamentally different ontology;
9. the environment generator does not admit observationally identical but semantically distinct target roles that require an external convention to distinguish.

These are useful empirical assumptions. They are not an identifiability theorem for `Q`, `P`, and `d`.

## Guarantees actually established

The paper establishes empirical policy-learning and generalization results:

- manuals can improve policy transfer to held-out games;
- entity-conditioned attention outperforms compared baselines;
- latent language-to-entity/dynamics alignment can be learned using environment reward;
- compositional zero-shot behavior is possible on Messenger;
- the hardest reported stage remains substantially unsolved.

The paper does not prove:

- unique recovery of raw-expression equivalence classes;
- unique recovery of the latent intervention-target partition;
- unique recovery of the utterance-to-target denotation;
- elimination of entity, role, symbol, or reward-preserving permutations;
- identification under arbitrary observation encodings or arbitrary game generators;
- separation of semantic grounding from benchmark-schema and reward supervision;
- recovery of an ontology finer than the distinctions rewarded by Messenger.

## Prior-art boundary added to the matrix

The following claims are excluded from the novelty space:

- learning latent mappings between free-form manuals and entity symbols from reward;
- grounding textual entity descriptions without an explicit word-to-symbol dictionary;
- grounding descriptions of environment dynamics for control;
- using entity-conditioned multimodal attention for language-guided RL;
- zero-shot generalization to unseen combinations of known entities and dynamics;
- jointly learning a control policy and an implicit grounding sufficient for task success;
- treating manuals as a compositional interface to a family of generated games.

Therefore, RQ-001 cannot claim novelty merely by learning a reward-sufficient latent alignment between language and state entities or by demonstrating held-out game success.

## Boundary between functional grounding and identification

Messenger demonstrates **functional grounding**: the learned representation supports successful behavior on a benchmark family.

RQ-001 asks for a stronger property: whether the available law uniquely determines:

1. which raw expressions are equivalent;
2. which latent targets belong to the same intervention block;
3. which language class denotes which target block.

Task success identifies only distinctions required by reward, transition, and evaluation. Any semantic distinction invisible to those channels remains unconstrained.

Moreover, reward is an orientation anchor. It tells the learner which consequences count as success. That is legitimate task supervision, but it prevents interpreting the result as wholly unsupervised discovery of external meaning.

## Identifiability counterexample A: entity-role permutation

Consider two latent target roles `p1` and `p2`, two raw-expression classes `q1` and `q2`, and a Messenger-like generator.

Model A uses:

`d_A(q1)=p1` and `d_A(q2)=p2`.

For a non-identity permutation `sigma`, construct Model B by simultaneously permuting:

- latent role labels;
- rendered entity-channel assignments;
- manual templates or lexical classes;
- role-indexed transition rules;
- reward and termination interface;
- policy input/output bookkeeping;
- evaluation labels.

Model B uses:

`d_B(q_i)=p_{sigma(i)}`.

The two models can preserve:

- every rendered observation distribution;
- every manual distribution after corresponding recoding;
- every action-conditioned transition distribution;
- every reward and termination distribution;
- every policy value;
- every train/test success rate;
- every attention pattern up to the same channel permutation;
- every zero-shot combination score.

Yet the external assignment of language classes to target roles differs.

Therefore:

> Perfect Messenger performance and perfect cross-modal alignment do not uniquely identify denotation when entity, role, language, and environment interfaces admit a simultaneous recoding.

## Identifiability counterexample B: reward-equivalent semantic refinement

Let two candidate target meanings `p1` and `p2` induce identical observations, transitions, rewards, and terminal outcomes under every admissible action and game context.

Construct Model A:

- `u1` and `u2` are synonyms;
- both denote one target block `p`.

Construct Model B:

- `u1` and `u2` have distinct meanings;
- they denote distinct latent targets `p1` and `p2`;
- Messenger's complete interactive law makes `p1` and `p2` indistinguishable.

Both models preserve:

- all manuals;
- all observation-action trajectories;
- all transition and reward laws;
- all policy gradients and optimal policies;
- all success and generalization metrics;
- all language-shuffle effects computed on the same benchmark.

Nevertheless `Q_A != Q_B` and `P_A != P_B`.

Thus reward-equivalence and trajectory-equivalence do not imply semantic equivalence.

## Identifiability counterexample C: policy success without correct lexical equivalence

A policy may assign different internal embeddings to two genuine paraphrases while mapping both to the same successful action sequence. Another policy may collapse two semantically distinct manuals that happen to require the same policy on the observed game family.

Both may achieve identical success on every available episode. Therefore action and reward metrics do not directly score raw-language equivalence. A claimed recovery of `Q` requires an independent partition-level evaluation or a theorem that successful interaction separates every relevant language class.

## Consequence for language dynamics pretraining

Pretraining on manual text can improve lexical and compositional representations, but it does not remove the cross-system permutation. Even perfect prediction of the manual corpus determines only its internal language law. It does not select one mapping from language classes to Messenger roles unless the interaction channel contains a non-recodable anchor.

Consequently, combining language dynamics pretraining with EMMA-style reward grounding may improve sample efficiency without improving identifiability.

## Mandatory controls introduced by C056

Before RQ adoption, a Messenger/SILG/J-CRe3-style baseline must include:

1. language-only representation baseline;
2. language-blind state/action/reward baseline;
3. shuffled-manual baseline preserving manual frequency;
4. entity-channel permutation control;
5. role-label permutation control;
6. simultaneous manual/entity/role/interface permutation control;
7. reward-only and terminal-outcome-only leakage audit;
8. parser, generator template, entity inventory, and split leakage disclosure;
9. direct evaluation of `Q`, `P`, and `d` separately;
10. held-out intervention pairs that distinguish targets requiring the same training policy;
11. countermodels with identical policy value but different semantic partitions;
12. preregistration of which interaction removes which residual automorphism.

## Decision

**NARROWED BEYOND REWARD-SUFFICIENT ENTITY-AND-DYNAMICS GROUNDING — NOT ADOPTED**

Reason:

- free-form manuals can already be grounded to entities and dynamics from environment reward sufficiently for control and zero-shot combination generalization;
- this functional grounding is established prior art;
- reward, the benchmark generator, fixed action semantics, entity inventory, and split construction provide substantial task structure;
- simultaneous language/entity/role/environment recoding preserves the complete interactive law and policy metrics;
- reward-equivalent targets and policy-equivalent manuals can support different semantic partitions;
- the work gives no joint identifiability theorem for `Q`, `P`, and `d`.

## Surviving candidate after C056

> After recovering the finest language equivalence supported by legitimate language observations, the finest target partition supported by non-language interventions, and the strongest reward-sufficient manual-to-world alignment obtainable in Messenger-like interaction, determine whether a preregistered cross-system intervention law uniquely separates only the remaining policy- and reward-aliased classes while eliminating every manual/entity/role/environment recoding that preserves the complete language, transition, reward, and action laws.

This candidate is not adopted. A positive result must provide an interaction or external law that is not merely the reward orientation, entity inventory, generator template, parser, or target bookkeeping being claimed as discovered.
